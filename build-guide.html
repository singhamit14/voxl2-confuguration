  1. Stay on SDK 1.4 — verify, do not flash

    The stack you were shipped already does everything this build needs, and it is calibrated to your specific hardware. Upgrading buys documentation alignment and risks the one thing you cannot regenerate.

    An earlier draft of this plan opened with a flash to SDK 1.6.6. Checking the board rather than the changelog overturned that. Three things decide it:

    • Your ESC is already supported. voxl-esc 1.4.8 ships esc_params_generic_m0134_6.xml — the 6S board in stage 2 is known to the tooling you have. This was the one finding that could have forced an upgrade, and it did not.
    • PX4 1.14.0 is a complete multicopter stack. Nothing in stages 3–6 needs a newer one.
    • Your calibration is per-unit and irreplaceable. Stereo front and rear extrinsics and intrinsics, tracking intrinsics, and an IMU calibration all sit in /data/modalai. They were measured against your physical cameras at the factory and cannot be recreated without calibration targets and a rig.

    The board also carries 72 offline .deb packages in /data/voxl-suite-offline-packages, so the missing network route does not block installing anything from the 1.4 set.

    Back the calibration up anyway — it costs nothing and it is the only copy:

    # already done: 13 calibration files + PX4 params -> /home/amit/voxl/backup
adb pull /data/modalai /home/amit/voxl/backup/modalai
adb pull /data/px4     /home/amit/voxl/backup/px4

    Then confirm the stack is healthy and move straight to stage 2:

    voxl-version
systemctl is-active voxl-px4
ls /usr/share/modalai/voxl-esc-params/boards/
    When upgrading does become the right call

    Three triggers, none of which apply yet: you hit a bug fixed upstream; you post to the ModalAI forum and are asked to reproduce on a current SDK; or you add hardware newer than the 1.4 tooling knows — check /usr/share/modalai/voxl-esc-params/boards/ before buying. If that day comes, the target is SDK 1.6.6 with system image 1.8.08 (1.6.7 and 1.6.8 are QCS6490-only; 1.7 and 1.8 are alpha and beta). Download voxl2_SDK_1.6.6.tar.gz from downloads.modalai.com, run ./install.sh, answer preserve at the /data prompt, and do not interrupt it — a killed flash corrupts the filesystem.

  2. Pick the thrust path, then order

    Your ESC choice decides your airframe size, not the other way round.

    Your voxl-px4.conf is already set to ESC=VOXL_ESC and RC=CRSF_RAW, so the least-friction build follows that. The constraint is current: ModalAI's 4-in-1 is rated 10A+ per channel, which is a compact quad, not a 500mm hauler.

    Path A — VOXL ESC on UART Recommended

    Use the M0134-6 (2–6S, 5.5–26V, 10A+/channel). Matches your live 24.4V rail and your existing config, and gives you per-motor RPM feedback and current sensing that PX4 actually uses. Build a 250–350mm quad on 5–7″ props. Note the -6 suffix — the M0134-1 and -3 variants are 4S only and will not survive your 6S pack.

    Path B — VOXL 2 IO + third-party PWM ESC

    Add the M0065 I/O expander (8× PWM + S.Bus, 25×33mm, 4g) and drive any third-party 6S 4-in-1. Take this only if you want a heavier 450–500mm airframe with 30A+ motors. Costs you the RPM telemetry and needs ESC=VOXL2_IO_PWM_ESC plus a firmware check — boards sold before 2024 need a bootloader update over the debug header.

    Parts for Path A, assuming the APM power module already feeding your 24.4V rail stays:

    ModalAI uses JST GH keyed connectors throughout. The single most common way a build stalls for two weeks is having every board on the bench and no cable to join them.

  3. Wire the airframe

    Everything on these headers is 3.3V logic. There is no tolerance for 5V on a signal pin.

    PortCarriesConnect
    J4Main power in, 5V + I2C battery monitoringAPM power module
    J18ESC UART @ 3.3VM0134-6
    J19GNSS UART + mag I2C + RC UART + 5VGPS/mag unit and CRSF receiver share this one
    J9USB-CADB to your PC — what you are on now
    J6 / J7 / J8Camera groups 0 / 1 / 2, 4-lane MIPI CSI eachAlready populated
    J2Fan control, 5V PWMCooling fan — fit one, the QRB5165 throttles hot
    J10External SPI expansionSpare

    J19 is the busy one: GNSS, magnetometer and RC all land on that single 12-pin header, so you will be using a breakout pigtail rather than plugging two things in.

    Verify before you plug

    Check this map against the VOXL 2 connector datasheet for your exact board revision before applying power. Confirm motor rotation directions and prop handedness on the bench in stage 5, not in the air.

  4. Configure PX4

    Your config already names the right hardware — this is confirming it against what you actually bolted on.

    Re-run the configure pass after the airframe is wired, then check the flight stack settings:

    voxl-configure-mpa
voxl-configure-cameras

# then edit /etc/modalai/voxl-px4.conf
AIRFRAME=MULTICOPTER
GPS=AUTODETECT
RC=CRSF_RAW          # CRSF_MAV if your RX speaks MAVLink
ESC=VOXL_ESC         # VOXL2_IO_PWM_ESC on Path B
POWER_MANAGER=VOXLPM

    Confirm the ESC is actually on the bus. This tool stops PX4 and disables its service to take the UART, so restart it afterwards — that is normal, not a fault:

    voxl-esc detect
voxl-esc calibrate

# restore PX4 when done
systemctl enable voxl-px4 && systemctl restart voxl-px4

    Then connect QGroundControl over the MAVLink bridge for airframe selection, sensor calibration and RC calibration. voxl-mavlink-server is already running on your board.

  5. Bench bring-up

    This is where you find the wiring mistakes, and the only safe time to find them.

    Props off — all of stage 5

    Remove every propeller before the battery goes in. A 6S quad that arms unexpectedly on a bench will take fingers. Props go on in stage 6 and not before.

    Walk the signal chain the same way we walked it today — each of these should now have a live publisher:

    px4-listener sensor_accel      # already good
px4-listener sensor_mag        # new — arrives with the GPS unit
px4-listener sensor_gps        # needs sky view for a fix
px4-listener input_rc          # timestamp_last_signal must be non-zero
px4-listener battery_status
px4-listener actuator_outputs

    RC is already done — an ELRS receiver is bound and reporting link_quality: 100 on J19. The tell is timestamp_last_signal, not whether the topic exists. Note that rssi: -1 and rc_total_frame_count: 0 are normal on CRSF and are not faults; see RCSETUP.md.

    Then, still with props off: spin each motor individually and confirm motor order and direction against your airframe's layout, check the mag for interference by watching heading while you throttle up, and verify VIO is tracking with voxl-inspect-qvio.

  6. First flight

    Short, low, outdoors, and with a plan for putting it down.

    • Fresh log enabled, battery failsafe and RC-loss failsafe configured and tested on the bench
    • Open space, no people, props inspected and torqued
    • First hover in Stabilized, not Position — you want to feel the trim before the position controller masks it
    • 30 seconds at one metre. Land. Pull the log and read it before the second flight
    • Only then move to Position mode, then Offboard for autonomy work

    Once it hovers cleanly, the cameras you already have become the interesting part: QVIO for GPS-denied position hold, and the stereo front pair for depth and obstacle avoidance through voxl-vision-hub.
